## Inspected material and interpretation

**IROn:** the 12-page paper was read, with particular inspection of §4's procedure and §§4.1–4.4, Figs. 2–3 (PDF p. 7) and Table 1 (§4.4, p. 10). Its parallel BFO/UFO analysis distinguishes a plan description from execution. Attack Scenario is treated differently across its foundational commitments, including a UFO situation-type interpretation and a BFO descriptive information-content interpretation. This is useful evidence that shared labels and alignment do not by themselves justify class equivalence. It does not identify IROn Attack Scenario, Security Incident or an LLM score as an equivalent of Riskium RiskCase.

The five-model, 21-classification exercise in §4.4 is explicitly preliminary. The paper places formalization in future work (§4 procedure). Its model scores are neither independent human expert validation of Riskium nor formal logical checks. Some classifications and source definitions receive differing judgments; no class or stereotype is adopted into Riskium merely because of a judge mean.

**Cybersecurity review:** focused full-PDF inspection covered the bibliographic header/Abstract, §3 and Table 1 (pp. 5–6), §4.2.5–4.2.6 and Table 6 (pp. 12–14), §4.3.2 (pp. 16–18), the results/discussion and §§7–9 (including pp. 35–39). Appendix listings are available, but the 93 primary studies, their individual artifacts and extraction scores were not independently re-reviewed.

The review's distinction among representation formalism, modeling paradigm and reasoning support (§4.3.2, p. 17) supplies a precise contextual claim for Theme 1. Its appraisal checklist can inform later evidence reporting; its composite scores are not calculated for Riskium. Its cybersecurity-specific scope does not establish a general organizational-risk gap. Figure 12 and §8 are explicitly a future integrated-security vision, not an implemented system demonstrated by Riskium.
