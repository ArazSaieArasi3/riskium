## When can we be confident?

There are three separate questions.

1. **Package identity:** v0.3.4's checksums/tag/archive and actual channels have verified observations. New source changes still need their own final-source evidence.
2. **Current traceability and consistency:** confidence is established at R3/R4/R5 for the chosen candidate: complete declared inventory, every retained claim connected to exact evidence, zero unexplained mismatches or critical retained defects, fresh actual manuscript/channel readbacks. After any affected edit, invalidate and rerun the affected checks.
3. **Scientific adequacy:** sound model interpretation, defensible novelty and external adequacy require actual sources and qualified independent judgments. A complete trace only shows what supports or limits a claim; it does not prove that interpretation correct.

A paper may be submission-ready before every broad issue, production deployment, PostgreSQL port, private migration or deferred mapping is completed, **if those results are not claimed** and P1–P6 are satisfied for the retained contribution. Release R6 and paper P1–P6 are tracked separately. Calendar timing depends on the selected venue, actual author/reviewer/source availability and discovered revisions; a repository test count cannot supply a credible deadline or acceptance guarantee.

## Reference criteria and limits

For a general artifact-quality reference, ACM's Artifact Review and Badging policy separates available artifacts, evaluated functional/reusable artifacts and independently obtained results. It emphasizes documentation, consistency with the paper, completeness relative to claims and exercisability. We use those distinctions as engineering guidance; **Riskium has not been awarded any badge** and the target venue may use different rules.

- [ACM Artifact Review and Badging, current policy](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/artifact-review-and-badging-current)
- [ACM Policy on Authorship](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/new-acm-policy-on-authorship)

The ACM authorship policy inspected for this definition was updated 14 May 2026 and distinguishes research use of AI from writing assistance. If an ACM venue is chosen, actual relevant research uses must be accurately described and human authors remain responsible. The final venue's current policies must be checked at P6; no target-specific compliance is asserted here.
